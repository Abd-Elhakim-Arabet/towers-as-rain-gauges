About this screen

Phone towers talk to each other over microwave beams, and rain weakens those beams. This screen shows the whole idea running on one city: the towers measure the rain, the rain goes into a flood model, and the flood model raises the alarm.

  • Real: tower positions and their operators (OpenCelliD; logos from Wikimedia Commons), terrain (Copernicus GLO-30), land cover (ESA WorldCover), soil (HYSOGs250m), buildings (OpenStreetMap), imagery (Esri), and the two storms (NASA IMERG, scaled to the rain gauge record).
  • Simulated: the signal of every link, the true rain at 500 m, and the flood itself. Algerian operators do not publish link data, so this is a simulation, not a measurement (Parts 4 and 5 of the explainer).
  • Rain sources: “True rain” is the simulated truth. “Towers + AI” and “Towers, classical” are what the links would measure, with our AI or the standard method. “Satellite only” is what exists today, available about 4 hours late.
  • Alarm: the flood model is run on the rain as it arrives. The alarm is raised when it shows the town flooding ( ha of built-up land under 30 cm), plus the time to get the data (towers 5 min, satellite 4 h).

Water colour: the realistic view draws muddy flood water whose colour deepens with depth; “Depth” and “Danger” use the same scales as the explainer (danger = depth × (speed + 0.5) + debris, UK DEFRA). Water thinner than 3 cm is not drawn.
